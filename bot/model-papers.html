Model Papers

MODEL PAPERS — new questions, not real past papers. Each paper follows the Dec 2025 pattern exactly: Section A 10 MCQs x 1 = 10 (all compulsory), Section B attempt any 4 of 6 short questions x 5 = 20, Section C attempt any 3 of 5 long questions x 10 = 30. Total 60 marks, 3 hours. No model-paper question is copied from the Dec 2025 paper.

Model Paper 1 — 60 marks, 3 hours

Section A — 10 x 1 = 10 (all compulsory)

A1. Water moves into a root cell when the cell's water potential is:

Answer: (b) Lower than the soil solution. Water flows from higher to lower water potential.

A2. Which process is responsible for the upward pull of water in tall trees?

Answer: (b) Transpiration pull. Transpiration pull, transmitted by cohesion, is the main force in tall trees.

A3. Sieve tube sap is rich in:

Answer: (b) Sucrose. Sucrose is the main transported sugar.

A4. The girdling experiment shows that organic food moves through:

Answer: (b) Phloem. Removing bark interrupts phloem and food accumulates above the girdle.

A5. P700 is the reaction centre of:

Answer: (b) Photosystem I. PSI reaction centre is P700; PSII is P680.

A6. The net ATP gain from glycolysis per glucose is:

Answer: (a) 2. Gross 4 ATP minus 2 invested = net 2 ATP.

A7. A competitive inhibitor increases the apparent:

Answer: (b) Km. More substrate overcomes competition; apparent Km rises, Vmax unchanged.

A8. Nitrite is reduced to ammonia in the:

Answer: (b) Chloroplast/plastid. Nitrate reduction is cytosolic; nitrite reduction is plastidic.

A9. The hormone that promotes seed dormancy is:

Answer: (c) Abscisic acid. ABA maintains dormancy and closes stomata in stress.

A10. Phytochrome Pfr is formed when Pr absorbs:

Answer: (b) Red light. Red light converts Pr to active Pfr; far-red reverses it.

Section B — Attempt any 4, 4 x 5 = 20

  1. Define water potential and explain solute and pressure potentials.
    Marking points: Definition; Ψ=Ψs+Ψp; pure water reference; examples of flaccid/turgid cells.
  2. Give the roles of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium in plants.
    Marking points: N in proteins/chlorophyll; P in ATP/nucleic acids; K in stomata/enzyme activation.
  3. Distinguish apoplastic and symplastic phloem loading.
    Marking points: Routes, membranes crossed, transporters, plasmodesmata, examples.
  4. Write a short note on photorespiration.
    Marking points: RuBisCO oxygenase, phosphoglycolate, three organelles, CO2 loss, C4 avoidance.
  5. Write a short note on enzyme inhibition.
    Marking points: Competitive, non-competitive, uncompetitive; Km/Vmax effects; feedback inhibition.
  6. What is vernalization? Give one example.
    Marking points: Cold requirement, winter cereals, devernalization, difference from photoperiodism.

Section C — Attempt any 3, 3 x 10 = 30

  1. Describe the cohesion-tension theory of ascent of sap and compare root pressure.
    Marking points: Transpiration pull, cohesion/adhesion, evidence, limits of root pressure, guttation contrast.
  2. Discuss the pressure flow model of phloem translocation with experimental evidence.
    Marking points: Münch model, source-sink gradient, aphid stylets, tracers, girdling, objections.
  3. Describe the Calvin cycle and explain how C4 plants reduce photorespiration.
    Marking points: Three phases, RuBisCO, Kranz anatomy, PEP carboxylase, bundle-sheath CO2 concentration.
  4. Describe biological nitrogen fixation, including nitrogenase and its energy cost.
    Marking points: Rhizobium nodules, leghaemoglobin, MoFe/Fe proteins, 16 ATP, O2 sensitivity, GS-GOGAT link.
  5. Explain photoperiodism and the role of phytochrome in flowering.
    Marking points: SDP/LDP/day-neutral, critical night, Pr/Pfr interconversion, night-break experiments, florigen concept.

Model Paper 2 — 60 marks, 3 hours

Section A — 10 x 1 = 10 (all compulsory)

A1. A cell placed in a hypertonic solution will:

Answer: (b) Lose water and plasmolyse. Water leaves toward the lower water potential outside.

A2. Which element is needed for chlorophyll synthesis and is a micronutrient?

Answer: (b) Iron. Fe is a micronutrient cofactor for chlorophyll synthesis.

A3. Phloem loading is an example of:

Answer: (b) Active transport. Sucrose is actively loaded against its gradient.

A4. Companion cells are found beside:

Answer: (b) Sieve tube elements. They support sieve elements, which lack nuclei at maturity.

A5. Cyclic photophosphorylation occurs around:

Answer: (b) PSI only. Cyclic flow returns electrons to PSI, making ATP only.

A6. The TCA cycle begins when acetyl-CoA joins:

Answer: (b) Oxaloacetate. Acetyl-CoA + oxaloacetate gives citrate.

A7. An enzyme's active site is shaped by:

Answer: (b) Its amino-acid sequence and folding. Structure determines binding and catalysis; induced fit refines the fit.

A8. Free-living nitrogen fixation is carried out by:

Answer: (b) Azotobacter in soil. Azotobacter is a classic free-living fixer; Rhizobium is symbiotic.

A9. Gibberellins are best known for:

Answer: (b) Breaking seed dormancy and stem elongation. GA promotes germination, bolting and internode elongation.

A10. A day-neutral plant flowers:

Answer: (c) Regardless of day length. Day-neutral plants are not controlled by photoperiod.

Section B — Attempt any 4, 4 x 5 = 20

  1. Explain root pressure and guttation, and how they differ from transpiration.
    Marking points: Definitions, hydathodes, conditions, limits, table of differences.
  2. Write short notes on: (a) aquaporins (b) ionophores.
    Marking points: Water channels, homotetramer idea; valinomycin, gramicidin, mobile vs channel types.
  3. Compare C3, C4 and CAM plants.
    Marking points: First product, anatomy, water use, examples, photorespiration.
  4. Explain the GS-GOGAT pathway.
    Marking points: Glutamine synthetase, glutamate synthase, ammonia assimilation, comparison with GDH.
  5. Mention the physiological roles of cytokinins.
    Marking points: Cell division, senescence delay, shoot/root balance, apical dominance interaction.
  6. What are Pr and Pfr? How do they interconvert?
    Marking points: Absorption maxima, red/far-red cycle, active form, photomorphogenesis examples.

Section C — Attempt any 3, 3 x 10 = 30

  1. Explain water potential and its components, with examples of cell states.
    Marking points: Ψ, Ψs, Ψp, Ψm note; flaccid, turgid, plasmolysed; water movement rule.
  2. Describe glycolysis and anaerobic respiration, including energy yields.
    Marking points: Steps in outline, net 2 ATP, fermentation types, NAD+ regeneration.
  3. Discuss enzyme catalysis: activation energy, induced fit, and factors affecting activity.
    Marking points: Transition state, Km idea, temperature, pH, substrate concentration, inhibitors link.
  4. Describe nitrate and ammonia assimilation in plants.
    Marking points: Uptake, cytosolic nitrate reductase, plastid nitrite reductase, GS-GOGAT, amino acids.
  5. Describe the Z-scheme and the generation of ATP and NADPH.
    Marking points: PSII to PSI carriers, photolysis, chemiosmosis, cyclic vs non-cyclic.

Model Paper 3 — 60 marks, 3 hours

Section A — 10 x 1 = 10 (all compulsory)

A1. Diffusion pressure deficit is an older term related to:

Answer: (a) Water potential. DPD reflects the suction pressure of a cell; modern usage prefers water potential.

A2. Boron deficiency in plants affects mainly:

Answer: (b) Growing tips and reproductive development. B is needed for cell-wall and reproductive growth; tips die back.

A3. Phloem sap pH is typically:

Answer: (b) Alkaline (around pH 8). Phloem sap is alkaline, unlike acidic xylem sap.

A4. Source-to-sink flow in phloem is driven by:

Answer: (b) A turgor-pressure gradient. Pressure at the source exceeds pressure at the sink.

A5. Carotenoids in photosynthesis act as:

Answer: (b) Accessory pigments and photoprotection. They harvest light and protect chlorophyll from excess energy.

A6. In CAM plants, malic acid accumulates:

Answer: (b) At night. Night fixation stores CO2 as malate; it is released by day for the Calvin cycle.

A7. Non-competitive inhibition lowers:

Answer: (b) Vmax. Vmax falls; the inhibitor does not compete for the active site.

A8. Leghaemoglobin is found in:

Answer: (b) Root nodules. It protects nitrogenase from free oxygen in nodules.

A9. Auxin is transported in plants mainly:

Answer: (b) Polarly, from shoot tip downward. Polar auxin transport is basipetal in shoots and distinctive among hormones.

A10. Vernalization in winter wheat ensures flowering after:

Answer: (b) A cold winter period. Cold makes the plant competent to flower when days lengthen.

Section B — Attempt any 4, 4 x 5 = 20

  1. Explain the significance of transpiration.
    Marking points: Ascent, cooling, mineral uptake, limits and costs, antitranspirants note.
  2. Write a short note on the composition of phloem sap.
    Marking points: Sucrose, amino acids, hormones, minerals, pH, sampling evidence.
  3. Distinguish photophosphorylation: cyclic vs non-cyclic.
    Marking points: Products, oxygen, carriers, ATP/NADPH balance.
  4. Explain the role of nitrogenase and why it needs protection from oxygen.
    Marking points: MoFe and Fe proteins, ATP cost, leghaemoglobin, anaerobic strategies.
  5. Give the roles of ethylene in plants.
    Marking points: Triple response, ripening, senescence, abscission, Ethephon.
  6. Differentiate SDP, LDP and day-neutral plants with examples.
    Marking points: Critical night, examples, night-break test.

Section C — Attempt any 3, 3 x 10 = 30

  1. Describe transpiration pull and root pressure, and assess their roles in ascent of sap.
    Marking points: Cohesion-tension, evidence, root pressure limits, tall-tree argument.
  2. Explain oxidative phosphorylation and chemiosmotic theory.
    Marking points: ETC, proton gradient, ATP synthase, inhibitors/uncouplers briefly, ATP yield.
  3. Discuss the pressure flow model: mechanism, evidence and objections.
    Marking points: Münch, source-sink, aphid stylets, tracers, bidirectional flow problem.
  4. Describe the mechanism of enzyme catalysis and the main types of inhibition.
    Marking points: Active site, transition state, competitive/non-competitive/uncompetitive, Km/Vmax.
  5. Explain phytochrome, Pr/Pfr interconversion and photomorphogenesis.
    Marking points: Discovery outline, structure, red/far-red cycle, germination, de-etiolation, flowering.

Model Paper 4 — 60 marks, 3 hours

Section A — 10 x 1 = 10 (all compulsory)

A1. Stomatal opening is promoted by:

Answer: (b) Potassium uptake into guard cells. K+ uptake lowers guard-cell water potential, water enters, and stomata open.

A2. Which is a micronutrient?

Answer: (c) Molybdenum. Mo is needed in tiny amounts, e.g. for nitrate reductase.

A3. Unloading at a sink requires:

Answer: (b) Energy for metabolism and growth. Sinks use imported sucrose for growth, respiration or storage.

A4. The pressure flow model predicts sap moves:

Answer: (b) From high to low turgor pressure. Bulk flow follows the pressure gradient from source to sink.

A5. The oxygen released in photosynthesis comes from:

Answer: (b) Water. Photolysis of water at PSII releases O2.

A6. The oxidative pentose phosphate pathway produces:

Answer: (b) NADPH and ribose-5-phosphate. It supports biosynthesis and nucleotide synthesis.

A7. An apoenzyme plus its cofactor forms a:

Answer: (b) Holoenzyme. Holoenzyme is the catalytically active complete enzyme.

A8. Ammonia assimilation by GS-GOGAT produces:

Answer: (b) Glutamate/glutamine amino acids. Ammonia enters organic form as glutamine, then glutamate.

A9. Cytokinins delay:

Answer: (b) Leaf senescence. Cytokinins keep leaves green longer and promote division.

A10. Red light converts phytochrome:

Answer: (b) Pr to Pfr. Red (660 nm) drives Pr → Pfr; far-red reverses it.

Section B — Attempt any 4, 4 x 5 = 20

  1. List the factors affecting transpiration and explain two in detail.
    Marking points: Light, temperature, humidity, wind, leaf area, stomatal state, water availability.
  2. Write a short note on the girdling experiment.
    Marking points: Method, result, conclusion, limitation (xylem intact).
  3. Explain the glyoxylate cycle and its importance.
    Marking points: Glyoxysomes, bypass steps, fat to carbohydrate in oil seeds.
  4. Compare competitive and non-competitive inhibition.
    Marking points: Binding site, Km, Vmax, reversibility by substrate, examples.
  5. Mention the roles of gibberellins.
    Marking points: Elongation, germination, malting, bolting, sex expression note.
  6. Explain photoperiodism with SDP and LDP examples.
    Marking points: Critical night, night-break, examples, phytochrome link.

Section C — Attempt any 3, 3 x 10 = 30

  1. Explain the concept of water potential, its components, and water movement between cells.
    Marking points: Definitions, Ψs/Ψp, examples, plasmolysis, turgor.
  2. Describe photosynthesis: Z-scheme, ATP synthesis and Calvin cycle in outline.
    Marking points: Carriers, chemiosmosis, three Calvin phases, ATP/NADPH use.
  3. Describe the TCA cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.
    Marking points: Matrix reactions, carriers, ETC, ATP synthase, yields.
  4. Discuss biological nitrogen fixation and ammonia assimilation.
    Marking points: Nodules, nitrogenase, energy, leghaemoglobin, nitrate/nitrite steps, GS-GOGAT.
  5. Explain plant growth regulators: auxins and ethylene in detail.
    Marking points: Discovery, transport/biosynthesis, roles, applications, triple response, ripening.

Model Paper 5 — 60 marks, 3 hours

Section A — 10 x 1 = 10 (all compulsory)

A1. Water potential becomes more negative when:

Answer: (b) Solutes are added. Solutes lower Ψs and therefore Ψ.

A2. Chloride is classified as a:

Answer: (b) Micronutrient. Cl is needed in small amounts, e.g. in photolysis and stomatal function.

A3. Aphid stylet exudation supports the idea that phloem sap is:

Answer: (b) Under positive pressure. Sap exudes from cut stylets, showing positive turgor pressure.

A4. Phloem loading through plasmodesmata is called:

Answer: (b) Symplastic. Symplastic route avoids membrane crossing until the sieve element.

A5. Chlorophyll b differs from chlorophyll a mainly in:

Answer: (b) Absorbing a different light range as an accessory pigment. Chl b broadens absorption and passes energy to Chl a.

A6. Fermentation of glucose to ethanol yields a net of:

Answer: (a) 2 ATP. Only glycolytic ATP is gained; NAD+ is regenerated without oxygen.

A7. Feedback inhibition helps a cell to:

Answer: (b) Avoid overproduction of an end product. End products inhibit early enzymes in their own pathway.

A8. Nitrogen fixation converts N2 mainly to:

Answer: (b) Ammonia. Nitrogenase reduces N2 to ammonia, later assimilated into amino acids.

A9. ABA promotes:

Answer: (b) Stomatal closure. ABA is the key water-stress signal for stomatal closure.

A10. A light break during a long night will:

Answer: (b) Prevent SDP flowering. Breaking the long night with light prevents short-day induction.

  3. Differentiate C4 and CAM pathways.
    Marking points: Anatomy, timing, first product, water use, examples.
  4. Write a short note on factors affecting enzyme activity.
    Marking points: Temperature, pH, substrate, inhibitors, cofactors.
  5. Mention the roles of auxins in plant growth.
    Marking points: Apical dominance, rooting, parthenocarpy, phototropism, weed killers.
  6. Explain vernalization and devernalization.
    Marking points: Cold duration, examples, reversal by heat, flowering competence.
