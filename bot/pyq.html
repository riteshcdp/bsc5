Previous Year Paper — December 2025

Booklet NEE-3107-A · BSc / BSc BEd / ITEP BSc BEd, Fifth Semester, End Semester Examination, Dec 2025 · Botany, BOT-DSM-511 (Plant Physiology and Metabolism) · Time: 3 hours · Maximum marks: 60

The paper was bilingual (Hindi and English). This transcription is in English. Note on the cover: the question paper is divided into three sections; attempt the questions as per direction.

Section A — Objective Type Questions (10 x 1 = 10)

Choose the correct answer. Each question carries 1 mark. All 10 are compulsory.

  1. The movement of water from cell to cell is mainly due to differences in: (a) Osmotic pressure (b) Water potential (c) Root pressure (d) Diffusion pressure deficit
  2. Guttation occurs through: (a) Stomata (b) Hydathodes (c) Lenticles (d) Cuticle
  3. Which of the following is a micronutrient? (a) Nitrogen (b) Phosphorus (c) Magnesium (d) Zinc
  4. Which pigment acts as the primary electron donor in photosynthesis? (a) Chlorophyll b (b) Carotene (c) Chlorophyll a (d) Xanthophyll
  5. In glycolysis, one molecule of glucose produces: (a) 2 ATP (b) 4 ATP (c) 6 ATP (d) 8 ATP
  6. Assimilation of ammonia into amino acids is catalyzed by: (a) Nitrate reductase and nitrite reductase (b) Glutamate dehydrogenase and glutamine synthetase (c) Nitrogenase and ATPase (d) Hydrogenase and peroxidase
  7. Short-day plants flower when: (a) Day length is shorter than the critical period (b) Day length is longer than the critical period (c) Night length is shorter than the critical period (d) Temperature is high
  8. Phloem loading involves: (a) Active transport of sucrose into sieve tubes (b) Passive diffusion of ions (c) Osmosis of water (d) Root pressure
  9. Enzymes are mainly composed of: (a) Proteins (b) Carbohydrates (c) Lipids (d) Nucleic acids
  10. Nitrate reduction in plants takes place in: (a) Cytoplasm (b) Mitochondria (c) Chloroplast (d) Nucleus

Section A answer key

1 (b) Water potential — water moves from higher to lower water potential.   2 (b) Hydathodes — guttation is not through stomata.   3 (d) Zinc — nitrogen, phosphorus and magnesium are macronutrients.   4 (c) Chlorophyll a — the reaction-centre pigment and primary electron donor.   5 (a) 2 ATP net — gross production is 4 ATP, 2 ATP are used up.   6 (b) Glutamate dehydrogenase and glutamine synthetase (GS-GOGAT route).   7 (a) Day length shorter than the critical period, as framed by this paper — in modern terms, short-day plants respond to a long uninterrupted night exceeding a critical length.   8 (a) Active transport of sucrose into sieve tubes.   9 (a) Proteins.   10 (a) Cytoplasm — nitrate reductase is cytosolic; the next step, nitrite reduction, is in the chloroplast/plastid. Do not confuse the two.

Section B — Short Answer Type Questions (attempt any 4 of 6, 4 x 5 = 20)

Each question carries 5 marks.

  1. Explain the concept of water potential and discuss its components. [Unit 1]
  2. Write short notes on: (a) Role of macronutrients in plant growth (b) Components of phloem sap. [Units 1 + 2]
  3. Differentiate between C3, C4 and CAM pathways of photosynthesis. [Unit 3]
  4. Write a short note on the mechanism of enzyme catalysis. [Unit 4]
  5. Mention the role of auxins in plant growth. [Unit 5]
  6. What are the Pr and Pfr forms of phytochrome? How do they interconvert? [Unit 5]

Section C — Long Answer Type Questions (attempt any 3 of 5, 3 x 10 = 30)

Each question carries 10 marks.

  1. Describe the process of transpiration pull and its role in the ascent of sap. How does it compare with the contribution of root pressure? [Unit 1]
  2. Describe the process of biological nitrogen fixation. Explain the role of nitrogenase enzyme, the mechanism of nitrogen reduction and energy requirements. [Unit 4]
  3. Discuss the pressure flow (mass flow) model of translocation in phloem. Explain the mechanism of source-to-sink transport and provide experimental evidence supporting this theory. [Unit 2]
  4. Describe the Z-scheme of electron transport in photosynthesis. Explain the sequence of electron carriers and the generation of ATP and NADPH. [Unit 3]
  5. Explain the mechanism of oxidative phosphorylation. Describe the role of the electron transport chain and chemiosmotic theory in ATP synthesis. [Unit 3, respiration]

Pattern analysis

  • Total: 10 + 20 + 30 = 60 marks, 3 hours. Choice exists only inside Section B (4 of 6) and Section C (3 of 5); Section A has no choice.
  • Section A is broad and factual: definitions, one-step facts, names, sites and numbers. It rewards short notes and flashcards, not long essays. Every unit is tested even here: Unit 1 = Q1–Q3, Unit 3 = Q4–Q5, Unit 4 = Q6, Q9, Q10, Unit 2 = Q8, Unit 5 = Q7.
  • Section B is roughly one question per major area, including two two-part short-note questions (B2). Answers need structure — definition, key points, example — about 1 to 1.5 pages each at exam speed.
  • Section C is mechanism-heavy: transpiration pull, nitrogen fixation, pressure flow, Z-scheme, oxidative phosphorylation. Four of the five long questions ask for a mechanism plus evidence or comparison, not just a definition.
  • Unit weight in this one paper (counting set questions, not attempted marks): Unit 1 is strong in A, B and C1; Unit 3 has two long questions plus B3 and A4/A5; Unit 4 has three MCQs plus B4 and C2; Unit 2 has A8, B2(b) and C3; Unit 5 has A7, B5 and B6 but no long question in this paper. Do not conclude Unit 5 cannot appear as a long question in another year — it is in the syllabus and B5/B6 show it is examined.
  • Critical link to the class notes: almost every Section B/C topic in this paper — water potential, phloem sap, enzyme catalysis, phytochrome Pr/Pfr, transpiration pull, nitrogen fixation, pressure flow, Z-scheme, oxidative phosphorylation — was missing or only partial in the 46-page notes. Only auxins (B5) and the ATP-synthesis part of the Unit 3 questions were well covered. That is why this site teaches the full syllabus.
  • Exam technique implied by the paper: for Section C, a diagram plus a stepwise mechanism plus 2–3 lines of evidence or comparison is the scoring pattern; for Section B, a short structured answer with a definition, components or factors, and one example each.
